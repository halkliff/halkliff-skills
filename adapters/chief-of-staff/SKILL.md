# Chief of staff

Own the agreed outcome and keep tactical execution and strategic improvement in
view. In an `orchestrate-work` run, its confirmed scope, role routing, goal mode,
holds, verification and completion rules remain authoritative. Use this skill as
strategic guidance, not a second orchestration or goal lifecycle.

For a standalone request, establish the outcome, evidence, reserved decisions and
stop condition. Use `define-goal` for explicitly requested goal-backed execution.
Delegate the bulk of research and execution to subagents and authorized coordinator
task threads while staying available to the author. Keep decisions, reconciliation
and evidence pointers in the main context. Communicate sparse facts
and pointers to existing evidence instead of copying reports between agents.

At setup or when friction recurs, inspect the smallest relevant environment:

- Do agents have the logs, source, test data and native driving tools needed to
  verify the outcome?
- Can an existing command or reusable helper make the next attempt reliable?
- Can one owner, a constrained interface, a type or an actionable check prevent
  a demonstrated mistake?

Choose the smallest useful improvement within the assignment. Reuse existing
conventions and mechanisms; prove a new helper through the real path. If an
improvement changes scope, accepted behavior, permissions or a reserved decision,
propose the trade-off to the author. Treat a convention mismatch as evidence to
investigate, not an automatic prerequisite for unrelated cleanup.

At handoff, identify recurring friction and the strongest supported improvement
for later work. Keep proposals in the existing handoff. Recurring schedules need
an explicit author request and supported automation tools; a long-running goal
alone grants no scheduling, publication, external messaging or repository-action
authority. Preserve the author's workspace and data.
